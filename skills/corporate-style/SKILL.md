# Corporate Style

## Overview

The company voice for developer content. Accuracy is the constraint that dominates: the reader is a developer who will find any overstatement, so credibility comes from being precise, concrete, and honest about limits. This guide is advocate-agnostic. Any advocate writes the same corporate voice.

Pairs with `personal-style` (the advocate's own voice). An advocacy cycle produces one draft in each voice from the same notes, and they must not read as the same article rearranged.

## Core rules

- Lead with the problem or the payoff, never the feature name.
- Put working code within the first screen.
- Data first, opinion second. Lead with the number, then say what it means. One stat per paragraph maximum.
- Concrete over abstract: anchor every capability claim to a specific pain and a specific outcome.
- No unearned enthusiasm. State limits plainly. Readers find them anyway, and finding them alone is worse than being told.
- Every number and every quote carries a source. No magic numbers.
- Close with the specific next action, not a general invitation.

## Simplified Technical English

Apply the clarity rules of [Simplified Technical English](https://skybrary.aero/articles/simplified-technical-english-ste), the controlled language used for technical documentation:

- One idea per sentence. Keep sentences short, aim under about 20 words.
- Active voice. "Send the request," not "the request is sent."
- Present tense for description, imperative for steps ("Open the file", "Run the command").
- One term per concept, every time. Do not vary a technical term for stylistic reasons.
- Break up noun strings. "The latency budget for the audio stream" beats "audio stream latency budget."
- No ambiguous references. Every "it" and "this" needs a clear referent.
- Prefer simple, common words over elaborate ones.

## Banned words

Never: leverage, delve, nuanced, robust, comprehensive, seamlessly, elevate, streamline, supercharge, empower, unlock. No enterprise-marketing speak (transformation, synergy, best in class). No breathless AI hype. If AI is involved, be specific about where and how.

## Formatting

- No em dashes. Use periods, commas, or restructure the sentence.
- Numbered steps for procedures, prose for context.
- Link out to related content rather than re-explaining it.
- A user's words beat your claims.

## Common mistakes

- Opening on the feature name instead of the pain.
- Three stats stacked in one paragraph. One per paragraph.
- Hedged enthusiasm ("incredibly powerful"). Say what it does.
- The same concept called two different things across the piece.
